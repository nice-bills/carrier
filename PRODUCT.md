# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who need to pay or be paid where there is no signal: markets, buses,
rural stretches, crowded events where the network collapses. They hold the phone
out to someone standing next to them, often outdoors in daylight, often
one-handed, often glancing rather than reading. Many will be new to crypto and
should never need to know the word.

## Product Purpose

Carrier moves a signed payment from phone to phone over local radio. Whoever
carries it earns a share when any phone in the chain reconnects and settles it
on Solana. Success is a first-time user taking a payment from a stranger,
handing it on, and seeing it settle with their share paid, all within a couple
of minutes of opening the app.

## Brand Personality

Plain, physical, a little mischievous. The ranks are an epidemic on purpose
(Dormant, Exposed, Carrier, Vector, Superspreader). Copy says what literally
happens, in short sentences, without crypto vocabulary.

## Anti-references

- Crypto wallets: purple on black, neon accents, glowing orbs, glass cards.
- Fintech dashboards: stat tiles, hero numbers with gradient accents.
- Gamified apps that shout: confetti, bouncing badges, rainbow avatars.
- Anything that reads as generated: soft pills everywhere, blurred drop
  shadows, emoji as icons, uppercase eyebrows over every section.

## Design Principles

1. The payment is an object. It is the one saturated thing on screen, and it is
   picked up and carried, never just listed.
2. Colour carries state and nothing else: amber is value in motion, teal is
   settled or live.
3. Say what the radio and the chain actually do. Permissions, keys and public
   records are explained at the moment they matter, in one sentence each.
4. Teach by doing. The first run ends with a real handoff, not a slideshow.
5. Readable in sunlight: high contrast, large targets, no text on translucency.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Every drag has a button equivalent. Reduced motion replaces all
movement with instant state changes. Colour is never the only signal.
